What to Look For in a Medicare & Health Insurance Agent

Not every agent is the same, and the differences matter more in Medicare than in almost any other purchase you'll make. Before you give anyone your Medicare number or your prescriptions list, run them through the checklist below.

1. Licensing and Certifications You Can Verify

In Florida, every life and health agent must hold an active license with the Florida Department of Financial Services. Ask for the license number and look it up on the DFS licensee search — it takes about ninety seconds. Cypress Ridge operates under Florida Life & Health Agent License #W284917. Beyond the state license, look for annual AHIP Medicare + Fraud, Waste & Abuse certification and, if you're under 65 and shopping Marketplace coverage, HealthCare.gov (FFM) certification for the current plan year. An agent who isn't currently AHIP-certified cannot legally write a Medicare Advantage or Part D application with you.

2. Independent vs. Captive — This Is the Big One

A captive agent works for one carrier and can only show you that company's products. An independent agent holds appointments with many carriers and can compare them side by side. If you call a 1-800 number from a television ad, you are almost always speaking with a captive or a single-carrier call center. Cypress Ridge holds more than 25 carrier appointments, which means we can move you to a better-fitting plan without you having to start a new relationship from scratch.

3. Local Knowledge of Networks and Hospitals

A Medicare Advantage HMO plan with an excellent network in Tampa might exclude your Orlando cardiologist. This is the single most common and most expensive mistake we fix for clients. A genuinely local agent knows that AdventHealth, Orlando Health, Nemours, HCA Florida, and the Orlando VA operate differently, and knows which plans contract with which systems this plan year — contracts do change annually.

4. The Full Picture: Life Insurance Too

Medicare doesn't cover everything, and it doesn't cover final expenses. A good agent can look at your whole household situation — mortgage protection, term life for a spouse who still works, final expense coverage for burial costs — rather than only selling you the one product on the brochure. This is especially relevant for the many self-employed and gig workers in the Orlando area who don't have employer benefits.

5. How (and Whether) They Charge You

Medicare-related consultations, plan comparisons, applications, and annual reviews should cost you nothing. Agents are compensated by carriers via commission. If anyone asks for a broker fee, a monthly service charge, or a markup on your premium, walk away.

This is the most common path we walk in the Orlando market, and it's also the one where independent representation matters most. Because Advantage plans often carry a $0 premium, the marketing is loud and the differences are buried. An independent agent checks whether your cardiologist at AdventHealth and your rheumatologist at Orlando Health are both in-network, confirms your specific medications sit on Tier 1 or Tier 2 rather than Tier 3, and verifies whether the plan's referral rules require a gatekeeper. Free annual reviews matter here because formularies and networks change every January.

Pick #2 — Medigap Plan G Paired with a Standalone Part D Plan

Typical monthly premium: quoted individually; varies by carrier and age • Best for: clients who want to see any Medicare-accepting doctor in the country without referrals.

Medigap is the "predictable" choice. Once you pay the Part B deductible, Plan G covers substantially all of the remaining approved Medicare costs, and there are no networks. The trade-off is a real monthly premium and the need to carry a separate Part D drug plan. This path suits clients with frequent specialist visits, those with a second home, and anyone who simply does not want to think about copays during a hospital stay. Because Medigap pricing varies by carrier, by the age you enroll, and by the underwriting rules that apply outside your initial Medigap Open Enrollment window, this is a decision that should always be quoted rather than guessed.

Pick #3 — ACA Marketplace Coverage with Subsidy Calculation

Typical monthly premium: often $0–$50 after premium tax credits for many Central Florida households • Best for: self-employed and gig workers, early retirees, and anyone under 65 without employer benefits.

Orlando has a large freelance and hospitality workforce, and a surprising number of people are paying full price for Marketplace coverage because they assumed they wouldn't qualify for help. Income estimates, household size, and cost-sharing reduction tiers interact in ways that are easy to get wrong — and an incorrect income estimate can mean repaying credits at tax time. We are ACA Marketplace certified through HealthCare.gov every plan year, so we calculate the subsidy and the cost-sharing reduction correctly the first time, then revisit it if your income changes mid-year.

Pick #4 — Final Expense & Burial Insurance (Ages 50–85)

Typical monthly premium: estimates commonly range $35–$90 depending on age and health • Best for: clients who want funeral, cremation, and small medical bills covered without a medical exam.

Medicare does not pay for funerals, and the average Central Florida service with burial or cremation can run several thousand dollars. Final expense policies are simplified-issue, which means approval is usually based on health questions rather than a blood draw or physical. Because pricing varies so much by age at issue — a 58-year-old and a 74-year-old buying the identical benefit can pay dramatically different premiums — the single best money-saving move is to apply earlier rather than later. We also offer no-medical-exam and simplified-issue life options for ages 0–85.

Pick #5 — Small-Group Health Benefits for 2–50 Employees

Typical monthly premium: underwritten and quoted per group • Best for: Orlando small-business owners trying to retain staff without overpaying.

Group health is a retention tool, and we treat it that way. Small-business owners with 2–50 employees receive a complimentary benefits benchmark report comparing the current group plan to three alternatives — including how a defined-contribution or level-funded structure might land. We round out the package with dental, vision, critical illness, accident, hospital indemnity, and disability income options so a single injury doesn't wipe out a key employee's savings.

Whichever path you're weighing, the first conversation is identical: we collect your doctors, your prescriptions, and your budget, then we tell you plainly which of the five fits — including when the honest answer is "stay where you are."

Tier 1 — Medicare Advantage: Often $0 Monthly Premium

Many Medicare Advantage plans in the Orlando market carry a $0 monthly premium, with costs appearing instead as copays for doctor visits, hospital stays, and specialist care. A "free" plan is not automatically a good plan — a low premium paired with a narrow network can cost you far more in a single hospitalization than a slightly richer plan would have. Always compare maximum out-of-pocket limits, not just the premium.

Tier 2 — Medicare Supplement (Medigap): Varies by Carrier and Age

Medigap Plan G pricing differs by carrier, by the age at which you enroll, and by the rate structure the carrier uses. Because there is no single "market price," this is where an independent agent earns their keep: identical Plan G benefits can be priced very differently across carriers. We quote multiple carriers and show you the numbers rather than describing them.

Tier 3 — ACA Marketplace: Often $0–$50/Month After Credits

For many Central Florida households, premium tax credits and cost-sharing reductions bring Marketplace plans into the $0–$50 per month range. This is an estimate that depends entirely on your household income and size, which is exactly why we calculate it rather than guess.

Term life policies commonly start around $18–$25 per month for a healthy 35-year-old at $250,000 of coverage. Final expense policies typically range $35–$90 per month depending on age and health. Expert Tips: Insider Advice and Mistakes to Avoid

After fourteen years and thousands of Orlando enrollments, our team sees the same avoidable mistakes every enrollment season. Here are the ones that cost clients the most.

  1. Never give your Medicare number to a cold caller. Medicare-related fraud spikes during the Annual Enrollment Period. A legitimate agent will give you their license number and an office address before asking you for anything.
  2. Check your pharmacy, not just your medication. Formularies are tiered per plan and per pharmacy. A drug that's $12 at one pharmacy counter can be $90 at another under the very same plan.
  3. Don't switch plans for a gym membership. A fitness benefit is nice; losing access to your cardiologist is not. Network continuity outranks extras every time.
  4. Watch the Annual Enrollment Period dates. Medicare Annual Enrollment runs October 15 – December 7. Outside that window, changes are limited. Our office runs extended hours during this period — Monday through Saturday 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM, plus Sunday 12:00 PM to 5:00 PM — precisely because of how compressed the timeline is.
  5. Understand your free-look period. Florida law gives you a free-look window on most policies (typically 10–30 days depending on the product), during which you can cancel and receive a full refund of premium. Use it. Read the policy when it arrives, not six months later.
  6. Don't let a policy lapse for a premium you didn't notice. Set autopay or a calendar reminder on the day the draft hits. Reinstatement after a lapse can mean re-underwriting and higher rates.
  7. Review annually — even if nothing feels wrong. Carriers change formularies, networks, and premiums every plan year. A ten-minute review in October routinely saves clients hundreds of dollars annually.

One more piece of insider advice: ask your agent what happens after the sale. At Cypress Ridge, the assigned agent personally assists with filing a claim — you are not handed off to a 1-800 call center. Every client also receives a free annual coverage review, and we honor a 30-day policy review guarantee: if a new policy isn't the right fit, we re-shop it at no cost.
