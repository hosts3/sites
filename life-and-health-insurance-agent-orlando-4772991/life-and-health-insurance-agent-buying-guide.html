What to Look For: The Agent Evaluation Checklist

An agent is not a product. You can buy an identical term policy or the same Medigap Plan G from two different agencies and have wildly different experiences at claim time. Here's how to separate them before you hand over your Social Security number.

1. Licensing, credentials, and accountability

Every agent selling life or health insurance in Florida must hold an active Life & Health (2-40) license through the Florida Department of Financial Services. Ask for the license number — a legitimate agent gives it without hesitation. Beyond the baseline, these are the credentials that actually change your outcome:

Credential What It Proves Why It Matters to You
Florida Life & Health license Legally permitted to sell Non-negotiable baseline; verify the number in the DFS licensee search
AHIP Medicare certification Annual Medicare + Fraud, Waste & Abuse training Required to enroll you in Medicare Advantage or Part D; keeps drug-plan math accurate
ACA Marketplace (FFM) certification Certified to enroll through HealthCare.gov each plan year Protects your subsidy and cost-sharing reduction calculations
Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance Professional liability coverage Means there's recourse if an application error costs you coverage
NAIFA / FAIA membership Ethics code and continuing education A mild but useful signal of a career agent vs. a seasonal one

2. Carrier access: independent vs. captive

This is the single biggest factor in whether you get a competitive price. A captive agent works for one carrier and can only sell that company's products. An independent agent holds appointments with many carriers and can shop the market. Independent agencies typically hold 20-40 appointments; Cypress Ridge, for example, works with 25+ carriers across life, Medicare, and Marketplace lines.

3. How the agent gets paid — and whether you pay anything

For life, Medicare, and ACA Marketplace products, agents are compensated by the carrier, not by you. There is no broker fee, no premium markup, and no monthly service charge at a reputable agency. If someone asks for an upfront "application fee" or a percentage of your premium, walk away. Ask directly: "Do I pay you anything, ever?" The answer should be a clean no.

4. Service commitments worth demanding in writing

  • Zero-pressure guarantee: no obligation to buy during the first (or fifth) meeting.
  • Free-look period honored: Florida gives you 10-30 days to cancel a new policy for a full premium refund, depending on the product.
  • Annual review: rates, drug formularies, and ACA subsidies change every year. A one-and-done agent leaves money on the table.
  • Response time: "within one business day" is a fair standard — get it in writing.
  • Claims advocacy: your agent should personally help file the claim, not route you to a 1-800 number.

5. Local presence and meeting logistics

Looking for insurance in Orlando is genuinely easier when the agency is local. Central Florida specifics that matter: hurricane-season document access, Medicare Advantage networks built around AdventHealth, Orlando Health, and HCA Florida facilities, and agents who know which carriers are strong in Orange, Seminole, Osceola, Lake, Polk, and Brevard counties. Also confirm the meeting options — evening and in-home appointments until 8:00 PM Monday through Thursday are the difference between signing up and putting it off for another year.

Expert Tips: Mistakes to Avoid

  • Don't buy life insurance from your car insurance agent "because it's easy." Property-and-casualty agents often have one life carrier. One carrier means one rate class — and you may be leaving 40% on the table.
  • Never guess on your health history. An inaccurate answer on a life application can void a claim years later. Tell your agent about sleep apnea, cholesterol medication, past surgeries, and vaping — carriers have different appetites, and matching you to the lenient one is the agent's job.
  • Check your doctors before you check the premium. For Medicare Advantage and ACA plans, verify every physician and hospital on the plan's provider lookup. A $0 premium plan that excludes your cardiologist is the most expensive plan you can buy.
  • Re-shop Part D and ACA every single year. Formularies and subsidy calculations change annually. Fifteen minutes in October or November routinely saves $400-$1,200 a year.
  • Don't let a policy lapse for a missed payment. Set autopay. Reinstatement after 30 days often requires new underwriting.
  • Ask for the free-look period in writing. Florida law gives you a window to cancel for a full refund. A confident agent will point it out unprompted.
  • Watch out for pressure language. "This rate expires today" is almost never true for life insurance. Legitimate urgency exists in Medicare enrollment windows — not in a Tuesday afternoon term life quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does it cost me anything to work with an insurance agent?

No. In nearly every case — life, Medicare, ACA Marketplace, and ancillary products — the carrier pays the agent's commission directly. There's no broker fee, no premium markup, and no monthly service charge. If an agent quotes you a fee, that's your cue to call someone else. At Cypress Ridge, consultations, quotes, needs analyses, applications, annual reviews, and policy re-shopping are all free to the client.

How do I know if an agent is independent or captive?

Just ask: "How many carriers can you write with, and can I see the list?" An independent agency holds 20-40 appointments; a captive agent has one. Independents can also match your health profile to the most forgiving underwriter, which frequently lowers your rate class. If the answer is vague, assume captive.

How much life insurance do I actually need?

A common starting rule is 10-15x your annual income, adjusted for your mortgage balance, other debts, childcare costs, and years until your youngest child is independent. A 35-year-old earning $70,000 with a $300,000 mortgage and two young children usually lands between $750,000 and $1.2 million — often for under $60 a month in good health on a 20-year term. A needs analysis takes 15 minutes and costs nothing.

Can I get coverage if I have health problems?

Usually yes. Simplified-issue and no-medical-exam products are available for ages 0-85, and many carriers approve conditions like controlled diabetes, high blood pressure, and prior joint replacements at standard or slightly rated classes. Some agencies also use accelerated underwriting that skips the blood draw entirely. The key is having an agent who knows which carrier is lenient with your specific condition.

Is Medicare Advantage better than Medigap in Orlando?

Not universally — it depends on how you use care. Advantage plans often carry a $0 monthly premium and add dental, vision, and fitness benefits, but you stay in-network and pay copays as you go. Medigap Plan G has a higher monthly premium but lets you see nearly any provider nationwide who accepts Medicare, which is why snowbirds and frequent travelers often prefer it. Ask for a side-by-side comparison of both, including your specific prescriptions.

Can I meet in the evening or at my home?

Many Orlando agencies offer evening and in-home appointments Monday through Thursday until 8:00 PM, plus Saturday mornings by appointment. It's worth asking, because the alternative is often another year of no coverage. Virtual appointments via Zoom or phone are also widely available across Florida if you'd rather not meet in person.

What happens if I already have coverage from another agency?

Nothing bad — a good agency will review it for free. Ask for a complimentary policy review, which compares your current premiums, death benefit, riders, and beneficiaries against what the market offers today. Sometimes your existing policy is already competitive and the honest answer is "keep it." Other times you're paying $40 more per month for identical coverage, and switching takes one application.

Can I cancel a new policy if I change my mind?

Yes. Florida law provides a free-look period — typically 10 to 30 days depending on the product — during which you can cancel and receive a full refund of premium. A reputable agent will tell you this up front without being asked. It's the single easiest way to verify you're dealing with someone who isn't afraid of your second thoughts.
