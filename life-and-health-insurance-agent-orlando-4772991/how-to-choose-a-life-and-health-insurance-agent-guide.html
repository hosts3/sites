What to Look For in a Life and Health Insurance Agent

1. A Verifiable Florida License (Not a "Lead Generator")

Anyone quoting you a policy in Florida must hold an active Life & Health (2-15 or 2-18) agent license issued by the Florida Department of Financial Services. You can confirm this yourself at the DFS licensee search using the agent's name or license number. Ask for the number out loud. A legitimate agent gives it without hesitation — ours is #W284917.

Be aware that a large share of the people who call you are not licensed agents at all; they are appointment setters for a call center. They cannot answer coverage questions and they will not be there when you file a claim.

2. Independent Access to Multiple Carriers

A captive agent works for one insurance company and can only sell that company's products. An independent agent holds appointments with many carriers — 25+ in our case — which means they can actually shop your health history, budget, and county before recommending a plan. If an agent can only show you one brand's brochure, you are being sold to, not advised.

3. Both Life AND Health Under One Roof

Most families need term life, a Marketplace or Medicare plan, and possibly dental, disability, or a hospital indemnity policy. When one agent handles all of it, your beneficiary designations, underwriting assumptions, and renewal dates stay coordinated. Splitting life and health between two agencies is how people end up with a Medicare Advantage plan that excludes their specialist and a life policy that lapses during a move.

4. Local Availability and Real Appointment Options

In Orlando specifically, ask whether the agency offers in-home visits, evening appointments, Saturday hours, and bilingual service. Central Florida has large Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking communities, a growing snowbird population in Polk and Brevard counties, and shift workers at the theme parks and hospitals who cannot take a 10:00 AM weekday call. An agency that only operates 9-to-5 Monday through Friday will eventually cost you time.

5. Transparency About Who Pays Them

You should never be charged a broker fee, a monthly service charge, or a mark-up on your premium. Insurance agents are compensated by the carriers through commissions built into the rate — the same rate you would pay going direct. Ask directly: "Do I pay you anything?" The only acceptable answer is no.

6. Claims Help and Annual Reviews — Not One-and-Done Sales

The relationship matters most at claim time. Ask: "Will the same agent who sold me this policy help my spouse file the claim?" and "Will you review my coverage every year without me asking?" An agent who promises an annual review is protecting you from the two most common problems in this industry: overpaying after a rate change, and being underinsured after a raise or a baby.

Watch out for: "Independent" is sometimes used loosely. Ask for the carrier appointment list and a written quote from at least three carriers.

2. Captive Career Agent — Good for Simple, Brand-Loyal Buyers

Who it's for: Someone who already trusts a specific brand and only needs a straightforward 20-year term policy with no complications.

Why it can work: Captive agents know their own products deeply, and the carrier's brand recognition can feel reassuring.

The limitation: They cannot show you a cheaper option from a competitor — even when one exists. For healthy applicants under 40, we routinely find the same coverage for 15–35% less by shopping multiple carriers. Always ask a captive agent: "If you were me, would you look anywhere else?"

3. Online Marketplace or Call Center — Convenient, But Impersonal

Who it's for: Healthy applicants with uncomplicated needs who are comfortable self-serving online and reading plan documents themselves.

Why it can work: Fast quotes, no appointment, and easy online enrollment for a healthy 32-year-old who just needs $500,000 of term coverage.

The risk: Turnover is high and you often cannot reach the same representative twice — which is exactly when you need help: when underwriting asks a follow-up question, or when your spouse files a claim. If you use a call center for the purchase, keep a local agent's number for the annual review.

4. Medicare-Focused Specialist — Best for Ages 64½ to 75

Who it's for: Anyone turning 65, retiring early, or already on Medicare Advantage and frustrated with prior authorizations or a shrinking network.

Why it matters: Medicare has a hard deadline. Your Initial Enrollment Period around age 65 is the cleanest window — miss it and you may face late-enrollment penalties or a waiting period. An AHIP-certified agent should compare Part D formularies against your actual prescriptions, not just the premium.

Local advantage in Orlando: Networks differ dramatically by county. A plan that works in Winter Park may exclude your cardiologist in Kissimmee. Choose an agent who will check the provider directory for your ZIP code and your doctors.

5. Group Benefits Broker — For Small-Business Owners With 2–50 Employees

Who it's for: Orlando-area employers — restaurants, clinics, contractors, professional offices — trying to hire and retain staff without a Fortune 500 budget.

What to ask for: A benchmark of your current group plan against at least three alternatives, plus a look at whether a defined-contribution or ICHRA-style approach fits better. A good broker also offers key-person life coverage and buy-sell funding for partners.

Watch out for: Brokers paid heavily by one carrier, or renewals presented without a claims-utilization review. Ask for the loss ratio; it is your strongest negotiating tool.

What the "Tiers" Really Mean

  • No-fee tier (everyone): Consultation, quote, needs analysis, application, and annual review. Cost: $0. If an agent charges for these, walk away.
  • Budget tier: ACA Marketplace with premium tax credits, Medicare Advantage, term life, and hospital indemnity. This is where most Orlando households land.
  • Comprehensive tier: Medigap + Part D, term or permanent life, disability income, and critical illness stacked together. More pieces, still no fee to you — and a written annual review that keeps them aligned.
  • Business tier: Small-group health benefits, key-person life, and buy-sell funding. Priced by carrier and census; ask for a benchmark report before renewing.

Expert Tips and Mistakes to Avoid

After 14 years and thousands of Orlando policies, these are the errors we see most often:

  • Buying on premium alone. A $4-cheaper Medigap plan means nothing if the carrier raises rates aggressively at age 72. Compare the carrier's rating history, not just today's number. Same for life insurance: a cheap policy with a two-year contestability window works differently than one that's fully underwritten.
  • Skipping the annual review. Subsidies change, formularies change, and your life changes — new baby, new mortgage, new diagnosis. Ask for the review in writing each year, at no charge.
  • Leaving health details off the application. Non-disclosure is the #1 reason life claims get delayed or denied. Tell your agent everything; a good one knows which carriers underwrite sleep apnea, controlled diabetes, or a past back surgery more favorably.
  • Choosing an agent who sells only one carrier. One brochure is not a comparison. Insist on at least three written options side by side.
  • Forgetting the free-look period. Florida gives you a right to cancel a new policy within a set window (typically 10–30 days, depending on the product) with a full refund of premium. Use it if the policy doesn't match what you were shown.
  • Not asking who handles claims. If the answer is a 1-800 number, you have your answer. Choose an agent who files the claim personally with your family.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does it cost anything to work with a life and health insurance agent?

No. Consultations, quotes, needs analyses, applications, annual reviews, and claim assistance are free. Agents are paid commissions directly by the insurance carriers, and that commission is already built into the rate — you pay the same premium whether you work with an agent or buy direct. There are no broker fees, no premium mark-ups, and no monthly service charges.

How do I verify an agent is licensed in Florida?

Ask for their Florida Department of Financial Services license number, then look it up in the DFS licensee search. Ours is #W284917. You can also ask whether they are ACA Marketplace certified through HealthCare.gov for the current plan year and AHIP-certified for Medicare — those certifications must be renewed annually, so a current one tells you the agent is staying current.

What is the difference between an independent agent and a captive agent?

A captive agent works for a single insurance company and can only sell that company's products. An independent agent holds appointments with many carriers — Cypress Ridge holds 25+ — so they can compare rates, underwriting guidelines, and networks across companies. Independent agents are also free to re-shop your coverage at renewal if a better fit appears.

How much does term life insurance cost in Orlando?

As a rough estimate, a healthy 35-year-old can often find $250,000 of 20-year term coverage for about $18–$25 per month, and final expense policies typically run $35–$90 per month depending on age and health. Your actual rate depends on age, tobacco use, medical history, coverage amount, and term length. Always ask for a personalized quote rather than relying on a range.

Can I get a $0 monthly ACA Marketplace plan in Central Florida?

Many Central Florida households qualify for premium tax credits that bring Marketplace premiums to $0–$50 per month, and some also qualify for cost-sharing reductions that lower deductibles and copays.
