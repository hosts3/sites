The Six Questions That Reveal Everything

  1. "Are you independent or captive?" — Independent means they can move you to a better carrier next year.
  2. "How are you paid, and does it change based on which plan I pick?" — The honest answer names the commission range and admits that some plans pay more.
  3. "Who handles my claim?" — You want a name, not a 1-800 number.
  4. "Do you review my coverage annually?" — One-and-done sales are how people end up overpaying for a decade.
  5. "Which Orlando-area hospitals and doctors are in this network?" — Ask about AdventHealth, Orlando Health, and Nemours specifically.
  6. "What's the free-look period, and what happens if I change my mind?" — Florida law gives you 10–30 days depending on the product, with a full premium refund.

Every one of those answers is a legitimate part of the benefits of how to choose a life and health insurance agent Life and Health Insurance agent conversation. A good agent will answer them in plain language without flinching.

What "Free" Actually Means for the Agent

Transparency builds trust, so here's the math. On a term life policy, carriers typically pay the writing agent a first-year commission that is a percentage of annual premium, plus a smaller renewal percentage in later years. On Medicare Advantage, carriers pay a fixed per-enrollment amount set by CMS. On ACA Marketplace plans, carriers pay a per-member-per-month amount. None of it comes out of your pocket, and none of it changes your premium.

That said, commission levels vary by product — which is precisely why you should ask the question out loud. A straight answer is a green flag. Deflection is not.

Expert Tips: Seven Mistakes That Cost Orlando Households Real Money

  1. Buying life insurance without comparing your existing employer group coverage first. Group term is often cheap but not portable — it disappears when the job does.
  2. Choosing a Medicare Advantage plan by premium alone. In Orlando, a $0 plan that excludes your cardiologist costs far more than a $28 plan that keeps them.
  3. Skipping the free-look period review. Florida gives you 10–30 days to return a policy for a full refund. Read the delivered contract during that window, not six months later.
  4. Guessing your ACA income. Underestimate and you repay subsidies at tax time; overestimate and you leave money on the table. Ask your agent to model three income scenarios.
  5. Naming a minor child as a direct beneficiary. A court-supervised guardianship may control the money. A revocable living trust or a named adult trustee usually works better.
  6. Never revisiting coverage after a life event. A new baby, a mortgage refi, a diabetes diagnosis, or a move to Celebration or Davenport all change the right answer.
  7. Working with an agent who won't answer the phone in month nine. Ask for a documented response-time commitment in writing.

One more: don't wait until the Medicare Annual Enrollment Period (October 15 – December 7) to start shopping. Appointment calendars in Orlando fill up by mid-October. How is an independent agent different from a captive one?

An independent agent holds appointments with many carriers — Cypress Ridge has 25+ — and can compare them side by side. A captive agent sells one company's products only. The practical benefit shows up at renewal, when an independent agent can re-shop your coverage instead of telling you the increase is normal.

Can I get life insurance if I have health conditions?

Often yes. Simplified-issue and no-medical-exam products are available for ages 0–85, and many carriers price diabetes, hypertension, and prior cancer history individually. The key is applying to the right carrier first — a declined application stays on your record and complicates the next one.
