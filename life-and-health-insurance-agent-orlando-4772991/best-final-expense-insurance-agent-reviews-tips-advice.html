A worksite agent is useful for enrolling, but ask about portability in writing.

Pros: Low immediate cost, payroll convenience, no medical questions in many cases.
Cons: Not portable long-term, no customization, no help with Medicare or funeral-cost planning.

Best for: Employees supplementing — not replacing — individual final expense coverage.

Pros: Price locking, one-stop planning, easy for families who want everything decided.

Frequently Asked Questions About Choosing a Final Expense Agent in Orlando

How much does final expense insurance cost in Orlando?

Most Central Florida final expense policies run roughly $35 to $90 per month depending on age, health, tobacco use, and face amount. A 55-year-old in good health might pay closer to $40 for $15,000 of coverage, while a 75-year-old with a chronic condition could pay $85–$90 for the same benefit. These are estimates only — a real quote takes about ten minutes.

Does it cost anything to work with an independent agent?

No. Independent agents are paid commissions directly by the insurance carriers, so consultations, quotes, needs analysis, applications, and annual reviews are free to you. There are no broker fees, no premium markups, and no monthly service charges. If an agent ever asks you to pay a fee for a quote, walk away.

Can I get final expense coverage if I have a serious health condition?

Often yes. Simplified issue policies ask health questions but skip the medical exam, and guaranteed issue policies accept applicants ages 45–85 with no health questions at all — though they usually include a two-year graded benefit period. An independent agent earns their value here by matching your exact condition to the carrier with the most lenient underwriting guide.

What should I look for in reviews of final expense agents?

Ignore star averages alone. Read reviews that mention claims experiences, appointment punctuality, whether the agent explained underwriting classes, and whether they returned calls after the sale. Reviews mentioning "helped my mother file the claim" are far more meaningful than "signed up fast." Also check the Florida Department of Financial Services license lookup — Cypress Ridge's license number is W284917.

Can I have both Medicare and final expense coverage through the same agent?

Yes, and many Orlando clients prefer it. Medicare Advantage, Medigap, and Part D are health products; final expense and burial insurance are life products. A licensed Life and Health agent who carries both appointments can coordinate the two — for example, making sure a Medicare Advantage copay change does not crowd out a $45 monthly life premium.

Is there a free-look period in Florida?

Yes. Florida law gives you a free-look period on new life policies — typically 10 to 30 days depending on the product — during which you can cancel and receive a full refund of premium paid. Reputable agencies honor it without argument. Ask the agent to point out the exact window in your policy before you sign.

What if the agent I bought from leaves the industry?

This is the clearest argument for an agency with a team rather than a solo operator. Ask up front: who inherits my file, who files my claim, and how do I reach them? Agencies with service coordinators and multiple licensed agents — and that offer free annual reviews — protect you against turnover in a way a one-person operation cannot.

Do I have to buy on the first call?

Absolutely not. Any agency worth its license will tell you there is zero obligation to purchase during a consultation. A good first call should end with you understanding your options and your price range — not with a signature. Take the quote, sleep on it, and compare.

Practical Tips Before You Choose Any Final Expense Agent

  • Verify the license. Every Florida life agent has a license number. Look it up before your appointment.
  • Ask how many carriers they represent. Fewer than five means limited shopping; twenty or more means real options.
  • Get the quote in writing. Premium, face amount, benefit type (level vs. graded), and the contestability period.
  • Confirm the day-one coverage rules. Graded and modified policies may return only premiums plus interest if death occurs in the first two years.
  • Name a primary and contingent beneficiary. Never leave it blank — the payout then goes to your estate and can be delayed by probate.
  • Ask about the annual review. Rates and health classes change; a good agent re-checks your coverage once a year for free.
