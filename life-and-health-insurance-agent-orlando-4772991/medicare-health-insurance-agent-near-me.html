What a Medicare Agent Actually Does (Beyond Handing You a Brochure)

Medicare has four moving parts — Part A (hospital), Part B (medical), Part C (Medicare Advantage) and Part D (prescription drugs) — plus a fifth option most people hear about but don't understand: Medigap, also called Medicare Supplement. Choosing wrong in month one can mean living with a network you don't like for the next twelve months.

A competent agent walks through these steps with you, usually across one or two conversations:

  • Provider and pharmacy check. We look up every doctor, specialist, hospital and the exact pharmacy you use, then verify they're in-network for each plan being considered. In Orlando this matters enormously — AdventHealth, Orlando Health, Nemours and HCA facilities do not all appear in the same plan networks.
  • Drug formulary review. Your specific medications are checked against each Part D or Advantage formulary for tier placement, prior authorization requirements and quantity limits. A $47 drug on one plan can be $310 on another.
  • Cost modeling, not guessing. We estimate your realistic annual out-of-pocket total — premiums plus copays plus the maximum out-of-pocket cap — instead of comparing monthly premiums alone.
  • Enrollment paperwork and verification. We submit the application, confirm the CMS acknowledgement, and follow up until you receive your card and member ID.
  • Ongoing service. Annual Notice of Change letters in September, drug plan re-checks every fall, and a real person when a claim is denied.

That last point is the one people underestimate. Nationally, roughly one in five Medicare Advantage prior-authorization requests is initially denied, and those denials are frequently reversed on appeal when someone who knows the process files the paperwork correctly. An agent who knows your file is worth more in that moment than any savings comparison spreadsheet.

Neither column is "better." The right answer depends on your doctors, your medications, your travel habits and your tolerance for network rules. Anyone who tells you one product is always the answer is selling, not advising.

Your Enrollment Timeline: Dates That Cost Money If You Miss Them

  • Initial Enrollment Period (IEP): the 3 months before your 65th birthday month, the birthday month itself, and the 3 months after — seven months total. Enroll in the first three months and coverage starts on your birthday month. Wait until the last three and you can face gaps and delays.
  • Annual Enrollment Period (AEP): October 15 – December 7 every year. This is when you can switch Advantage plans, move between Advantage and Medigap, or change Part D plans. Changes take effect January 1.
  • Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment: January 1 – March 31. One change allowed if you're already in an Advantage plan.
  • General Enrollment Period: January 1 – March 31 for people who missed their IEP and don't qualify for a Special Enrollment Period. Coverage begins July 1 and late penalties may apply.
  • Special Enrollment Periods: triggered by losing employer coverage, moving, or qualifying for Medicaid/Extra Help.

One trap worth naming: if you're still working past 65 and covered by an employer group plan of 20+ employees, your Medicare timeline shifts — and Medigap guarantees work differently. That's a conversation worth having with an agent before you sign anything.

Seven Questions to Ask Any Agent Before You Say Yes

  1. "Are you independent or captive?" A captive agent can only offer one carrier's products. An independent agency like ours holds 25+ carrier appointments.
  2. "What is your Florida license number, and are you AHIP certified?" Ask them to state it out loud. Ours is #W284917, and every Medicare-writing agent here re-certifies annually through AHIP plus CMS Fraud, Waste & Abuse training.
  3. "How much do I pay you?" The correct answer is nothing — carriers pay commissions, and there should be no broker fee, premium markup or monthly service charge.
  4. "Will you check my doctors and drugs, or just my ZIP code?" If they skip this step, walk away.
  5. "Who handles my claim?" You want a named agent, not a 1-800 call center. Our claims-help guarantee means your assigned agent files it with you.
  6. "What happens after I enroll?" The right answer includes an annual review each fall at no charge.
  7. "Can I think about it?" If you feel pressured, that's your answer. Our zero-pressure guarantee means no obligation to purchase during any consultation.

Expert Insights From 14 Years of Writing Medicare Policies in Central Florida

Cypress Ridge Life & Health Insurance was founded in 2011. In that time our team of nine licensed agents and two client-service coordinators has watched the same handful of mistakes repeat — and they're almost always avoidable.

  • Don't shop on premium alone. A zero-premium Advantage plan with a $6,700 in-network maximum out-of-pocket is not cheaper than a $40 Medigap premium if you have a cardiac event in March. Model the worst year, not the best one.
  • Medigap underwriting is age-sensitive. In Florida, you generally have guaranteed-issue rights during your initial Medigap Open Enrollment Period (six months starting when Part B begins at 65). After that, health questions may apply. Buying later is often more expensive or unavailable.
  • Re-check your Part D plan every October. Formularies change annually. Clients who never review often find a medication moved to a higher tier the following January.
  • Keep the Annual Notice of Change letter. That thin booklet arriving in September tells you exactly what's changing. Bring it to your review — it saves twenty minutes.
  • Watch out for "Medicare"-branded marketing. Unsolicited calls, fake government websites and door-knockers are common in the Orlando metro. A legitimate agent will give you a license number, a physical address and a written scope of appointment.

Our standard: no decision has to be made on the first call. We'd rather you take a week, talk to your spouse, and call back certain.
