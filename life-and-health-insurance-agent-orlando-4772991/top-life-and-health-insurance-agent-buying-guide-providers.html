What to Look For in a Life and Health Insurance Agent

Most people shop for insurance backwards. They search a carrier name, get routed to a call center, and never learn there were twelve other carriers willing to cover them for less. The better approach is to shop for the agent first and let the agent shop the carriers second. Here is what actually separates a good Orlando insurance agent from a mediocre one.

1. Independent vs. Captive: The Single Biggest Factor

A captive agent works for one insurance company and can only sell that company's products. An independent agent holds appointments with many carriers and legally can compare them. Cypress Ridge holds 25+ carrier appointments, which is why a 38-year-old Orlando homeowner with controlled high blood pressure can often be placed with a different carrier than a 38-year-old with a clean health history — same coverage amount, noticeably different premium.

2. Licensing, Credentials, and Continuing Education

In Florida, anyone selling life or health insurance must hold an active license through the Florida Department of Financial Services. Ask for the license number and verify it. Beyond the minimum, look for Medicare-writing agents who hold current AHIP certification, and Marketplace agents who complete HealthCare.gov FFM certification every plan year. These are not marketing badges — they are annual exams covering fraud, waste, and abuse rules, enrollment compliance, and subsidy calculations.

3. How They Get Paid (And Whether It Costs You)

A legitimate independent agent is paid commissions directly by the insurance carrier. You should never pay a broker fee, a monthly service charge, or a premium mark-up. If an agent asks for a consultation fee, that is a red flag. Cypress Ridge charges nothing for consultations, quotes, needs analyses, applications, or annual reviews.

5. Things You Should Be Able to Name Before You Buy

Any agent worth their license should be able to tell you, in plain language: the exact monthly premium, the death benefit or out-of-pocket maximum, the free-look period length, whether the rate is guaranteed level for the full term, and what happens if you miss a payment. If those five answers are fuzzy, keep shopping.

Expert Tips and Mistakes to Avoid

  • Never let an agent quote you without asking about your medications. The cheapest advertised premium is meaningless if the plan does not cover your inhaler or your spouse’s insulin. Ask the agent to run the drug list against the formulary.
  • Do not cancel old coverage until the new policy is issued and delivered. A pending application is not coverage. Cypress Ridge schedules the cancellation of your old policy only after the new one is active.
  • Use the free-look period. Florida gives you a set window — typically 10 to 30 days depending on the product — to review a policy and get a full refund of premium if it is not right.
  • Be honest on the application. A misstated health history or smoking status is the single most common reason a legitimate claim gets contested years later.
  • Ask whether your rate can change. Term life is usually level for the term; Medicare Advantage and ACA rates adjust annually; Medigap rate schedules vary by carrier. Know which you are buying.
  • Schedule the annual review. Life changes — a new baby, a new mortgage, a new diagnosis, turning 65, losing a job. A policy that fit in 2021 may be overpriced or under-sized today.
  • Do not buy from an agent who will not put the comparison in writing. You should receive a document showing at least three options, not a single illustration.
