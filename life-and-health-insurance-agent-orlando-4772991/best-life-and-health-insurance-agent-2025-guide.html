Top Picks: 5 Types of Orlando Insurance Agents, Reviewed

There is no single "best agency" for everyone — but there is a best type of agent for your situation. Here is an honest breakdown of the five options Orlando residents actually choose from.

1. Independent multi-carrier brokerage — Best overall for most households

What it is: An agency appointed with 20–30+ carriers across life, Medicare, and Marketplace products. A local example is Cypress Ridge Life & Health Insurance on E. Robinson Street in downtown Orlando — founded in 2011, licensed under Florida DFS #W284917, with 9 licensed agents and 25+ carrier appointments.

Strengths: They can place a client with sleep apnea, type 2 diabetes, or a recent cardiac event with whichever carrier underwrites that condition most favorably. They handle life and health, which means one person sees the whole picture. They are paid by carriers, so your consultation, quote, application, and annual review cost $0.

Watch out for: Ask how many carrier appointments they truly hold, and whether the same agent who writes your policy will also service your claim.

2. Captive career agency — Good for brand familiarity, limited for shopping

What it is: A single-brand office (think one big mutual insurer). The agent can only quote that one company's rates and underwriting rules.

Strengths: Strong brand recognition, polished digital tools, and often excellent training.

Watch out for: A $250,000 20-year term policy can differ by 40%+ between carriers for the exact same person. If the quote feels high, it may simply be the wrong carrier for your health profile.

3. Medicare-only specialist — Excellent for age 65+, thin for families

What it is: An agency that writes only Medicare Advantage, Medigap, and Part D. During Orlando's Annual Enrollment Period (Oct 15 – Dec 7) they are extremely busy and extremely knowledgeable.

Expert Tips From 14 Years of Writing Orlando Policies

Cypress Ridge opened in 2011 and our owner, Marcus D. Whitfield, still writes policies personally. These are the mistakes we fix most often.

  • Never cancel old coverage before the new policy is issued. In life insurance, the new policy must be approved and delivered first. In Medicare, a new Advantage plan takes effect on a fixed date — one wrong date can leave you with a gap and a denied pharmacy claim.
  • Tell the truth on the application. A rescinded death claim because of an omitted inhaler prescription is the single most heartbreaking conversation in this industry. Full disclosure often still gets approved, just with the right carrier.
  • Ask about the free-look period. Florida requires a free-look window on most policies — typically 10 to 30 days depending on the product — during which you can cancel for a full premium refund.
  • Do not chase the lowest Medigap premium alone. Plan G benefits are identical by law; the difference is the carrier's rate-increase history over 10 years. An agent who shows you 10-year rate history is worth more than one who shows you the cheapest number today.
  • Watch the Florida Medicaid gap. Florida has not expanded Medicaid, so some adults between roughly 100% and 138% of the federal poverty level can fall into a coverage gap. If Healthcare.gov tells you that you qualify for no subsidy, ask an agent about alternative options before assuming you are uninsured.
  • Schedule the annual review. A plan that was right in 2022 may be wrong in 2025 — drug formularies change, rates change, and new carriers enter Orange and Osceola counties every January. Reviews should be free, forever.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an insurance agent cost me anything?

No. For life, health, Medicare, and ancillary products, the insurance carrier pays the agent's commission out of the premium it already charges. You pay the same price whether you use an agent or buy directly. There are no broker fees, no premium mark-ups, and no monthly service charges.

Why choose an independent agency instead of a single-brand office?

Because underwriting is not uniform. One carrier may decline obstructive sleep apnea while another offers a standard rate. An independent brokerage with 25+ appointments can place your case where it belongs. Do I have to buy anything during the consultation?

No. Every consultation at Cypress Ridge carries a zero-pressure guarantee — you are never obligated to purchase, and you can take the quote home and decide later. We also offer a 30-day policy review guarantee: if a newly written policy is not the right fit, we re-shop it at no cost to you.

Can I get life insurance without a medical exam?

Yes. Simplified-issue and no-medical-exam options are widely available for ages 0 to 85, and they cover everything from $5,000 final expense policies to $500,000 of accelerated-underwriting term coverage. Approval usually takes days instead of weeks, though pricing may be slightly higher than a fully underwritten policy for a fully healthy applicant.

What is the best time of year to sign up for health coverage in Orlando?

For ACA Marketplace plans, Open Enrollment runs November 1 through January 15, with coverage starting January 1 if you enroll by December 15. Medicare's Annual Enrollment Period runs October 15 through December 7. Outside those windows you generally need a qualifying life event — marriage, birth, job loss, a move, or losing other coverage. We offer extended hours during enrollment: Monday through Saturday, 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM, plus Sundays 12:00 PM to 5:00 PM.

Can you help if I already have a policy from another agency?

Absolutely. We offer a free 15-minute policy review to anyone who already owns life or health insurance elsewhere, with no obligation to change anything. Roughly a third of the policies we review are already competitive — and we say so honestly rather than pushing a replacement.

How do claims work if something happens to me?

Your assigned agent personally assists with filing the claim — not a 1-800 call center. That is a written commitment at our agency. We gather the death certificate, the claimant statement, and the carrier forms, and we follow up until the benefit is paid.
