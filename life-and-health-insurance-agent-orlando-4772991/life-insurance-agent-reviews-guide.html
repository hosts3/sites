2. Captive Career Agent — Single Brand

A career agent represents one company. Product knowledge can be excellent, and brand-name recognition is real. The limitation is structural: when underwriting declines you or rates you higher for a controlled blood-pressure medication, the agent has no second option to place you with.

Fits: Buyers who already trust one specific brand and need only a small term policy. Weak spot: simplified-issue and final expense cases where a single carrier's health questions screen you out.

Expert Tips: What Experienced Buyers Do Differently

  • Verify the license before the appointment. Look up the agent's name on the Florida Department of Financial Services license search. It takes ninety seconds and tells you whether the license is active, what lines it covers, and whether any disciplinary action exists.
  • Never decide on the first call. A legitimate agent will tell you that no purchase is required during a consultation. If you feel pressured to sign before you have read the illustration, that pressure is the review.
  • Ask "how many carriers can you place me with?" Twenty-five or more means genuine comparison shopping. One or two means a sales quota for a specific brand.
  • Use the Florida free-look period. Depending on the product, you typically have 10 to 30 days after delivery to cancel for a full refund of premium. Read the policy when it arrives, not when you need it.
  • Beware the "one-size" recommendation. The same $250,000 term policy can differ by hundreds of dollars a year between carriers based on your build chart, medications, and driving record. A good agent asks about all three.
  • Schedule the annual review. Coverage bought for a $180,000 mortgage in 2019 may be badly out of step with a $310,000 mortgage and two kids in 2025. Cypress Ridge offers this review at no charge to every client.

The most common mistake is not overpaying — it is buying from an agent who cannot be reached when a claim is filed. Test responsiveness before you buy. Send an email with a question and time the reply.

How do I know whether an agent's online reviews are real?

Look for specificity. Genuine reviews mention a product type, a claim experience, a named staff member, or a timeline. Be cautious of clusters of near-identical five-star reviews posted within the same week. Also cross-check: an agent with 30 reviews across Google, Facebook, and the Better Business Bureau is more credible than one with 30 reviews on a single platform.
