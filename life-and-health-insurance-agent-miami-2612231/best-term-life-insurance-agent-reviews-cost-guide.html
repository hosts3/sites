Three Cost Tiers, Explained

Tier 1 — Entry ($0–$30/month). Level term coverage for younger, healthy buyers, plus $0-premium Medicare Advantage plans. At this tier, cost is almost entirely driven by age and health class; the agent’s job is to get you into the best class, not to find a “deal.” Consultations, quotes, policy reviews, and ACA or KidCare enrollment help should cost you nothing — commissions are paid by the carrier, not the client.

Tier 2 — Mid ($32–$160/month). This is where most Miami families land: $250,000–$500,000 of term coverage for parents in their 40s and 50s, or final expense and mortgage protection policies with living benefits for ages 45–85. Watch for riders — waiver of premium, accelerated death benefit, child riders — which add a few dollars each and are often worth it.

Tier 3 — Premium & Permanent ($160+/month). Whole life, Indexed Universal Life with cash value accumulation, and annuities such as Fixed Indexed or Multi-Year Guaranteed Annuities. These are long-horizon decisions, not impulse purchases. A responsible agent will show you the term alternative side by side and let you decide, and will never ask for a commitment at the first appointment.

4. Expert Tips & Mistakes to Avoid

  • Never buy on rate alone. The cheapest quote is meaningless if the carrier declines you after a paramedical exam and the agent never re-shops the case. Ask: “If I’m rated or declined, what happens next?”
  • Answer medical questions completely. A forgotten 2019 blood pressure medication is the most common cause of a contested claim. The two-year contestability period is real, and full disclosure is your protection.
  • Ladder your coverage. Rather than one $750,000 / 30-year policy you’ll overpay for in your 50s, stack a $500,000 / 20-year and a $250,000 / 30-year policy, then drop the smaller one when the mortgage is paid.
  • Do the math on replacement. Replacing an older policy only makes sense if the new one prices lower — and the old one is still within its contestability period. Ask for a written side-by-side and never cancel until the new policy is issued and the first premium is drafted.
  • Check the license yourself. Look up any agent on the Florida Department of Financial Services licensee search. A 2-15 license covers life, health, and annuities; a 2-40 covers health only. If someone selling you term life holds only a 2-40, walk away.
  • Book the free annual review. Beneficiaries change. So do incomes. A ten-minute yearly check has caught outdated ex-spouse beneficiaries on more Miami policies than any other single issue.
  • Avoid the “no one will call me” trap. Digital-only purchases are fine until a claim is filed. Confirm in writing who will help your family file paperwork before you sign — and get that person’s direct phone number.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Does it cost anything to work with a Life and Health Insurance agent?

No. Independent agents are compensated by the insurance carrier, not the client. Consultations, quotes, policy comparisons, and annual reviews should all be free with no obligation. If anyone asks you to pay a fee, a “processing charge,” or an application deposit directly to the agent, that is not standard practice — decline and get a second opinion.

How many agent reviews should I read before choosing someone?

Volume matters more than stars. Fifty reviews is a bare minimum; 200+ is a meaningful sample. Read the three-star reviews first — that’s where you learn about response times and post-sale service. Then check whether the praise is specific (“she explained why my rating changed”) rather than generic. A Miami agency with 300+ reviews and a 4.9 average, like Biscayne Bay Life & Health Insurance Group, has enough history to spot patterns across years rather than weeks.

What’s the difference between an independent agent and a captive agent?

An independent agent holds appointments with multiple carriers and can shop your case across all of them — which matters most when you have a health condition that one carrier rates harshly and another ignores. A captive agent works for one company and can only sell that company’s products. Neither is automatically dishonest; the difference is how many options you get to compare.

Do I need a medical exam for term life insurance?

Not always. Fully underwritten policies typically require a paramedical exam, blood draw, and urine sample, which usually produces the lowest rates. Simplified-issue and accelerated-underwriting products use prescription history, motor vehicle records, and database checks instead, and can be approved in 24–72 hours. Guaranteed-issue products for ages 50–85 require no health questions at all, though the benefit is often graded for the first two years.

Can I get coverage if I’ve been declined before?

Yes, in most cases. A decline from one carrier is not a decline from the industry. Carriers publish different underwriting guides — one may decline a history of sleep apnea or a treated cardiac condition while another approves it at a standard rate. This is exactly where a multi-carrier independent agent earns their keep: they read the guides before submitting, so you don’t collect multiple declines on your record.

How long does it take to get a term life policy in Miami?

Simplified-issue policies can be issued in one to three business days. Fully underwritten policies typically take two to six weeks from application to approval, and the clock starts once the medical exam is completed, not when you apply. The single biggest delay is applicant scheduling — book the exam within 48 hours of applying.

Should I buy life insurance and health insurance from the same agent?

It’s convenient and often smart, provided the agent is licensed for both. A Florida 2-15 license covers life, health, and annuities, so one agent can handle term life, ACA Marketplace coverage with subsidy calculations, Medicare Advantage or Medigap, and Part D drug plans. Having one point of contact for annual reviews — especially if you’re approaching 65 — reduces the chance that a coverage gap goes unnoticed.

What if I already have a policy and just want it reviewed?

A policy review is free and does not require you to switch anything. A good review covers four things: whether the coverage amount still matches your income and debts, whether the term length still aligns with your mortgage and children’s ages, whether your beneficiary designations are current, and whether the same coverage is now available at a materially lower rate. If nothing should change, a trustworthy agent will tell you exactly that.
