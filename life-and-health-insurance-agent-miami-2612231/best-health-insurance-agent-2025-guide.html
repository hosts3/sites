The Six Practical Questions That Reveal a Great Agent

  1. "Are you captive or independent?" An independent agent can move you between Florida Blue, Ambetter, Molina, and Oscar as your income or doctors change. A captive agent can only sell one brand's book.
  2. "Will you check my doctors and prescriptions before recommending a plan?" In Miami, Baptist Health, Jackson Health, University of Miami Health, and Memorial Healthcare each sit in different networks. Plan documents must be checked line by line.
  3. "What happens when I have a claim problem?" The strongest agencies handle carrier calls and paperwork for you — especially for beneficiaries filing a life insurance claim during a difficult time.
  4. "Do you charge me anything?" In Florida, health and life agents are compensated by the insurance carrier. You should never pay a fee for a quote, an enrollment, or an annual review.
  5. "How fast do you respond after enrollment?" A 24-hour response guarantee is the difference between a professional agency and a seasonal enrollment shop.
  6. "Will you review my policy every year?" Formularies change, networks shrink, and premiums reset. A free annual review is the single most valuable service an agent provides.

Best for: members who are already loyal to a single brand and rarely switch providers.

3. Medicare-Only Specialist Agency — Deep but Narrow

These agencies live inside the October 15 – December 7 Annual Enrollment Period and often run free educational workshops. They are excellent at Medicare Advantage vs. Medigap comparisons, Part D drug-tier analysis, and CMS-compliant enrollment. They typically cannot help a 38-year-old Uber driver find an ACA subsidy or a young family buy a 20-year term policy. Best for: turning-65 retirees who also have a life insurance need already handled elsewhere.

4. National Online Call Center — Fast, Impersonal

You will find these agencies at the top of paid search with slick quote engines. Enrollments are fast and the websites are polished, but the agent who enrolled you frequently disappears by February. There is no Little Havana or Kendall office to walk into, no Spanish-speaking case manager who remembers your mother's prescriptions, and no one advocating on a denied claim. Best for: experienced buyers who only need a transaction and will self-manage afterward.

5. Solo Neighborhood Generalist — Personal but Capacity-Limited

A one-person shop in Hialeah, Sweetwater, or Westchester can offer wonderful personal attention and true bilingual warmth. The risk is capacity: during Medicare AEP, a solo agent handling 300 clients may take three days to return your call when a prescription prior authorization stalls. Ask how many active clients they service and whether they have backup staff. Best for: clients who value a single familiar face above all else.

Expert Tips and Mistakes to Avoid

  • Never share your Medicare number to "check eligibility" over a cold call. CMS prohibits unsolicited Medicare calls. If someone phones you out of the blue about Medicare, hang up and call a licensed agent you initiated contact with.
  • Do not buy the lowest premium before checking the drug formulary. A $0 Medicare Advantage plan that does not cover your inhaler or insulin can cost $3,000 more per year than a plan with a small premium. Miami-Dade formularies vary by carrier and by year.
  • Do not miss your Medigap Medically Underwritten window. In Florida you generally have a six-month window beginning the month you turn 65 and enroll in Part B. Outside that window, health questions apply — and a single Miami cardiology visit can affect pricing.
  • Estimate ACA income carefully. Subsidies are based on projected household income, not last year's tax return. 1099 contractors and gig drivers in Miami frequently under- or over-estimate and end up reconciling at tax time.
  • Buy term life before you need it, not after. A health event at 42 can double the price of a 20-year term policy at 45. Lock the rate while underwriting is still favorable.
  • Never cancel an existing policy before a new one is issued. Wait for the carrier's approval letter and the first premium draft. This is the single most common and most painful mistake.

Frequently Asked Buying Questions

Does it cost anything to work with a life and health insurance agent in Miami?

No. Consultations, quotes, enrollments, and annual policy reviews are free because the insurance carrier pays the agent's commission. At Biscayne Bay Life & Health Insurance Group, that includes a free policy review even for coverage you bought somewhere else.

Can an agent get me a lower price than Healthcare.gov shows?

The plan premiums are identical whether you enroll on the Marketplace or through a licensed agent — the difference is in the subsidy calculation and plan selection. An experienced agent will model your household income correctly, apply premium tax credits properly, and compare Bronze, Silver, and Gold options across Florida Blue, Ambetter, Molina, and Oscar so you are not overpaying for a tier you do not need.

What is the difference between a captive and an independent agent?

A captive agent represents one insurance company and can only sell that company's products. An independent agent holds appointments with many carriers — health, Medicare, and life — and can move you to a different plan as your income, doctors, or prescriptions change. Independence matters most at renewal, when a network or formulary shifts.

When should I enroll in Medicare if I turn 65 this year?

Enroll in Part A and B during your Initial Enrollment Period, which begins three months before your 65th birthday month and ends three months after. If you want a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan for the following January, you can compare options during the Annual Enrollment Period from October 15 to December 7. If you or your spouse still have employer coverage, your timing may be different — review that first.

Do I need a medical exam to get life insurance?

Not always. Fully underwritten term policies for $250,000 to $2,000,000 typically involve a paramedical exam, bloodwork, and a prescription check. Simplified-issue and Guaranteed Issue policies for ages 50–85 may require no exam and no health questions at all, though the price per thousand of coverage is higher. Final expense coverage for ages 45–85 also has no-exam options.

Can I keep my current doctors when I switch plans?

That depends entirely on the network. In Miami-Dade, Baptist Health, Jackson Health, University of Miami Health, and Memorial Healthcare participate in different plans, and a physician may be in-network for one Medicare Advantage plan and out-of-network for another. Always give your agent your doctor list and prescription list before enrollment, and confirm participation in writing from the carrier.

I already have a policy. Can an agent still help me?

Yes, and at no cost. A free policy review covers beneficiary updates, cash-value performance on whole life or IUL policies, whether a term policy is still adequate after a new mortgage or a new baby, and whether a Medigap rate increase justifies shopping for identical coverage. If a client finds a lower rate for identical coverage, the agency will match it or explain the difference in plain language.
