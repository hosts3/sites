Frequently Asked Questions

How much does life insurance cost for a 30-year-old in Miami?

A healthy 30-year-old non-smoker typically pays roughly $18–$24 per month for $250,000 of 20-year term coverage, and about $28–$40 per month for $500,000. Women generally pay 10–25% less than men for identical coverage. Rates are locked for the full term, so buying at 30 rather than 40 can save thousands over the life of the policy.

Do I need a medical exam to qualify?

Not always. Many carriers now offer accelerated or simplified underwriting for applicants under 45 with clean health histories — no blood draw required. If you have a chronic condition, a fully underwritten policy usually delivers a better rate than a guaranteed-issue product. An independent agent can tell you which path gets you approved at the lowest premium.

Should I choose term or IUL as a young adult?

Term is usually the right starting point for most people in their 20s and 30s: it delivers the largest death benefit for the lowest premium during the years your family depends on your income. Indexed universal life makes sense once your term needs are covered and you have surplus cash flow you want to grow tax-deferred. Many of our clients in Miami do both — a 20-year term for protection and a smaller IUL for long-term cash value.

I'm self-employed with no employer benefits. Does that hurt my application?

No. Life insurance underwriting focuses on health, age, and lifestyle — not on whether you have a W-2. Self-employed 1099 contractors, rideshare drivers, restaurant workers, and salon professionals are among our most common clients. You will simply verify income on the application rather than submit employer paperwork.

Can I get coverage if I've been declined before?

Often, yes. A decline from one carrier does not mean every carrier will decline you — underwriting guidelines vary widely. Guaranteed-issue whole life (ages 50–85, no health questions) is available for those who cannot qualify elsewhere, and simplified issue products cover many conditions that fully underwritten policies exclude.

What happens to my policy if I move out of Florida?

Your policy travels with you. Life insurance is not tied to your state of residence — the premium and coverage remain the same if you relocate to another state. Our agency is licensed statewide in Florida, so if you move within the state we continue to service your policy; if you leave Florida, we will help you transfer servicing to a licensed agent in your new state.

How long does approval take?

No-exam products can be approved in 24 to 72 hours. Fully underwritten policies that require labs and medical records generally take two to four weeks. During the process your agent can request a temporary insurance agreement in many cases, so your family is covered while underwriting finishes.

Does it cost anything to work with a local agent?

No. Consultations, quotes, annual policy reviews, and beneficiary updates are completely free. Commissions are paid by the insurance carrier, not by you — the premium you pay is identical whether you buy directly from the carrier or through a licensed agent.
