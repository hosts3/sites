Frequently Asked Questions About Life and Health Insurance in Orlando, Florida

Answers updated for 2026 — including this year's Medicare Annual Enrollment dates, ACA subsidy changes and current underwriting timelines.

How much does life insurance cost in Orlando, Florida?

A healthy 30-year-old Orlando applicant can typically get $250,000 of 20-year term coverage for about $18–$24 per month, while a healthy 45-year-old can get $500,000 of 20-year term for roughly $58–$75 per month. Final expense policies for ages 45–85 generally run $32–$95 per month depending on age and whether you choose a no-exam option.

Do you charge a fee for your insurance services?

No. Every consultation, quote, comparison and annual policy review is 100% free with no obligation. Our agents are compensated by the insurance carriers rather than the client, which means there is never a fee for you to work with us and no financial reason for us to push one product over another.

How quickly will an agent respond to my quote request?

We back every request with a 24-hour response guarantee, and our average reply time for Orlando clients is under 4 business hours. Urgent Medicare enrollment deadlines, claim questions and same-day appointment requests are handled the same business day whenever possible.

How long does it take to get approved for life insurance?

Simplified-issue and no-exam policies often issue in 3 to 7 business days. Fully underwritten term and permanent policies typically take 2 to 6 weeks because of medical records, labs and the phone interview. We schedule every step for you and give you a realistic timeline up front rather than an optimistic guess.

Can you help me with Medicare when I turn 65 in 2026?

Absolutely. We compare Medicare Advantage (Part C), Medicare Supplement (Medigap) and Part D prescription plans from Florida Blue, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, Cigna, Molina and others. Many Medicare Advantage plans in the Orlando area are available at a $0 monthly premium. For Medigap Plan G, expect roughly $115–$210 per month depending on age and carrier.

What health insurance options do I have if I'm self-employed in Orlando?

Self-employed workers, 1099 contractors and gig drivers who have no employer benefits usually qualify for ACA Marketplace coverage with premium tax credits. Most Orlando households we enroll find their net premium falls below $50 per month after subsidies. We also help with Florida KidCare enrollment for children at no cost to you.

What happens if I have a health condition like diabetes or high blood pressure?

You still have strong options. Many carriers price controlled diabetes and high blood pressure at standard or slightly rated rates, and we know which carriers are most forgiving for each condition. If a fully underwritten policy isn't realistic, guaranteed issue life insurance for ages 50–85 requires no health questions at all.

Are you licensed to sell insurance in Orlando, Florida?

Yes. We hold Florida Department of Financial Services License 2-15 (Life, Health & Annuity) and License 2-40 (Health Insurance Only), are AHIP Medicare certified and CMS-compliant annually, are a member of NABIP, and have been A+ BBB accredited since 2015.

Can you review a policy I already own, even if you didn't sell it to me?

Yes — and we do it for free. Our no-obligation policy review covers death benefit amount, cash value performance, beneficiary designations, riders you may not know you have, and whether a newer 2026 product would cost less for identical coverage. If your current policy is better, we will tell you to keep it.

Do you offer coverage for small businesses with 5–50 employees?

We do. Our group benefits division builds health, life, dental, vision and disability packages for Orlando restaurants, salons, construction companies and professional offices with 5 to 50 employees. We handle carrier quotes, plan design and employee enrollment meetings so it doesn't become an HR burden.

What is the 2026 Annual Enrollment Period, and when should I act?

Medicare Annual Enrollment runs October 15 through December 7, 2026, and ACA Open Enrollment runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. We keep evening video appointments open Monday through Thursday until 8:00 PM during those windows because that is when most working families can actually talk.

Do you speak Spanish or Haitian Creole?

Sí — y wi. Our team includes three bilingual support staff, and our agents serve clients in English, Spanish and Haitian Creole. We also host a free bilingual Medicare workshop at our Little Havana office on the first Wednesday of every month, and we can conduct the entire enrollment process in your preferred language.

Why Choosing the Right Insurance Agent Matters in Orlando, Florida

Orlando is not an easy insurance market to navigate alone. Roughly one in five workers in the metro area is employed in hospitality, tourism or food service — industries that rarely offer employer-sponsored life coverage and often provide limited or expensive health benefits. Add a large population of rideshare and delivery drivers working as 1099 contractors, and you get thousands of Orange and Osceola county households shopping for coverage individually rather than through an employer. As of 2026, that means subsidy calculations, network comparisons and underwriting questions that a licensed local agent can resolve in a single conversation.

Zip codes 32801, 32803, 32806 and 32819 around downtown Orlando, Thornton Park and Dr. Phillips have very different provider networks than 34746 in Kissimmee or 32771 in Sanford. Neighborhoods near Lake Eola, Baldwin Park, College Park, Audubon Park and the Lake Nona Medical City corridor each sit in slightly different plan footprints, and a Medicare Advantage plan that works beautifully in Winter Park may exclude your cardiologist if you move twenty minutes south. Local agents catch those mismatches before you sign.

Our agency has served Florida families since 2011 and holds Florida Department of Financial Services licenses 2-15 and 2-40, plus annual AHIP Medicare certification and CMS-compliant marketing and enrollment. We are also required to follow Florida's replacement and disclosure regulations, which means any policy you already own gets compared on the record before we recommend a change — a protection out-of-state call centers frequently skip.

Three more Orlando-specific realities shape our advice. First, Florida has no state income tax, so tax-deferred cash value in an IUL or whole life policy behaves differently here than in states with high income tax. Second, hurricane season runs June through November, and we always review beneficiary designations and document storage before storm season so a claim is never stalled by lost paperwork. Third, many Orlando clients are dual-language households planning for extended family members, so we handle beneficiary updates, contingent beneficiaries and estate coordination conversations with sensitivity and in the language you're most comfortable using.
