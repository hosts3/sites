Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find the best life insurance agent near me in Miami?

Start by confirming the agent holds an active Florida 2-15 Life, Health & Annuity license through the Florida Department of Financial Services. Then check independence: an agent appointed with six or more carriers can rate-shop, while a captive agent sells one company. Finally, look for free consultations, a local office address, and verifiable reviews. In Miami, bilingual service is a practical advantage — insurance documents are dense, and understanding them in your first language prevents costly mistakes.

Does it cost anything to work with an independent life insurance agent?

No. Consultations, quotes, and policy reviews are free, and the agent's commission is paid by the insurance carrier — not by you. Your premium is the same whether you buy directly from the carrier or through an agent. The practical difference is that an independent agent can compare multiple carriers on your behalf.

What does term life insurance cost in Miami for a healthy adult?

As a general benchmark, a healthy 30-year-old can often get $250,000 of 20-year term coverage for roughly $18–$24 per month, and a healthy 45-year-old can get $500,000 of 20-year term for approximately $58–$75 per month. Final expense policies for ages 45–85 typically run $32–$95 per month for $5,000–$25,000 of coverage. Actual rates depend on age, health history, tobacco use, and the carrier.

Can I get life insurance without a medical exam?

Yes. Simplified-issue and guaranteed-issue policies skip the exam and often skip health questions entirely. Guaranteed issue is generally available for ages 50–85 with coverage typically capped around $25,000 and a graded benefit period for the first two years. If you're healthy, fully underwritten term coverage usually costs less per thousand dollars of protection — so it's worth comparing both.

I'm self-employed with no benefits. What should I prioritize?

Miami has one of the largest self-employed and 1099 workforces in the country. If you have no employer benefits, prioritize in this order: (1) an ACA Marketplace health plan with premium tax credits — many Miami-Dade households pay under $50 per month after subsidies; (2) term life insurance sized to your income and dependents; (3) disability income insurance, since your income stops if you can't work. Small business owners with 5–50 employees can also explore group benefits.

When should I enroll in Medicare or ACA coverage in Florida?

Medicare Annual Enrollment runs October 15 through December 7 each year, and ACA Open Enrollment runs November 1 through January 15. Outside those windows you generally need a qualifying life event — marriage, a move, job loss, or a new baby. Medicare Supplement (Medigap) enrollment is different: your best pricing window is the six-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period that starts when you're 65 and enrolled in Part B.

Should I replace an existing life insurance policy?

Sometimes, but not often. Replacement can mean new contestability periods, higher rates at an older age, and lost cash value. A responsible agent will run a side-by-side comparison first and tell you honestly if keeping your current policy is the better move. Free policy reviews — including on policies bought elsewhere — are a good way to check.

What happens to my family if I die — who handles the claim?

Your beneficiary files a claim with the carrier, typically with a certified death certificate. A strong agent will handle the carrier calls and paperwork on your beneficiary's behalf during an already difficult time. Ask any agent you interview directly: "Will you personally manage the claim for my family?" The answer tells you a lot.
