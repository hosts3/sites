What to Look For in a Life & Health Insurance Agent

Before you compare personalities, compare credentials. An agent's license type determines what they are legally allowed to sell you, and their carrier appointments determine how many options you'll actually see.

1. License Classifications That Matter in Florida

Florida License What It Allows Why It Matters to You
2-15 Life, Health & Annuity Life insurance, health insurance, Medicare Advantage/Supplements, annuities One agent can coordinate your term policy, your ACA plan, and your retirement annuity — no handoffs.
2-40 Health Insurance Only Health, Medicare, and limited-benefit products — not life insurance If a 2-40 agent offers you life insurance, that's a red flag. Ask to see the license.
AHIP Certification Annual Medicare-specific training and compliance exam Required to legally enroll you in Medicare Advantage. Renews every year — ask when they last recertified.
CMS Compliance Governs how Medicare plans can be marketed and sold Protects you from pressure tactics. A compliant agent will never cold-call you about Medicare Advantage unprompted.

2. Independent vs. Captive Agents

A captive agent works for one carrier and can only quote that carrier's products. An independent agent holds appointments with multiple carriers and can shop rates across all of them. For life insurance, independence typically means the difference between seeing 6–8 underwriting options and seeing one. That matters because carriers price the same 45-year-old applicant differently based on build, blood pressure, driving record, and even nicotine use — a difference that can represent hundreds of dollars per year.

3. The Evaluation Checklist

  • Verifiable license. Look them up on the Florida DFS licensee search before your first meeting.
  • Multiple carrier appointments. Ask: "How many life carriers can you actually submit to?" A good answer is five or more.
  • Compensation transparency. Ask directly: "How are you paid, and does your recommendation change if I pick Plan A versus Plan B?"
  • Documented service promises. Response times, annual reviews, and claims advocacy should be in writing — not verbal.
  • Language access. In Miami, receiving a Medicare explanation in your first language isn't a luxury — it prevents expensive enrollment mistakes.
  • Local accountability. A physical office you can visit is leverage you don't have with a national call center.

Pricing Breakdown: What You Actually Pay

Here's the part most guides bury: in the vast majority of cases, you pay the agent nothing. Commissions are built into the premium by the carrier. The fair question isn't "how much does the agent cost," it's "does the agent's compensation structure change what they recommend?"

Service Client Cost Who Pays
Life & health consultation / quote $0 Carrier commission
ACA Marketplace enrollment help $0 Carrier / federal subsidy
Medicare Advantage or Medigap enrollment $0 Carrier / CMS
Annual policy review $0 (standard at most independent agencies) Carrier renewal commission
Fee-only planning engagement $150–$400/hour or flat fee You, directly

Real 2025 Product Price Ranges in Miami-Dade

  • Term life — healthy 30-year-old: $250,000 / 20-year term runs roughly $18–$24 per month.
  • Term life — healthy 45-year-old: $500,000 / 20-year term runs roughly $58–$75 per month.
  • Final expense / burial insurance (ages 45–85): typically $32–$95 per month for $5,000–$25,000 of coverage, with no-exam options available.
  • Medicare Advantage: many plans in Miami-Dade carry a $0 monthly premium — you still pay copays for services.
  • Medicare Supplement Plan G: generally $115–$210 per month depending on age and carrier.
  • ACA Marketplace: most Miami-Dade households qualify for premium tax credits; many end up paying under $50 per month after subsidies.
  • Guaranteed issue life (ages 50–85): no health questions, priced higher per $1,000 of coverage — a last-resort option, not a first choice.

Tier takeaway: If an agent quotes you a consultation fee for a standard term life or Medicare plan review in Miami, that's unusual. Independent agencies are compensated by carriers. Fee-only engagements are appropriate for complex estate work — not for a $250,000 term policy.

Expert Tips & Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Never buy from the first quote you receive. Underwriting differences between carriers on the same applicant can swing premiums 30–60%. Ask to see at least three carrier illustrations side by side.
  2. Don't lie on the application — but do ask about "field underwriting." A skilled agent pre-screens your health history against each carrier's guidelines before submitting, so you land with the underwriter most likely to approve you at the best rate class.
  3. Watch the "free lunch seminar" trap during Medicare season. CMS rules restrict how Medicare Advantage can be marketed at events. A compliant agent will take your information and follow up — they won't enroll you at the table.
  4. Check the enrollment window, not just the price. Miss your Medicare Initial Enrollment Period or the ACA Open Enrollment deadline and you may be locked out for months. A good agent calendars those dates for you.
  5. Confirm claims advocacy in writing. The single most valuable service an agency provides is handling the carrier phone calls and paperwork when a beneficiary files a claim. Ask exactly who does that and how quickly.
  6. Review your policy every year. Term conversions, new health conditions, a new baby, or a refinanced mortgage can all change what you need. Free annual reviews should be standard, not an upsell.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I verify an insurance agent's license in Florida?

Go to the Florida Department of Financial Services licensee search, enter the agent's name or license number, and confirm three things: the license is active, the class is 2-15 (Life, Health & Annuity) or 2-40 (Health only), and there are no disciplinary actions on record. It takes about 90 seconds and it's the single fastest way to filter out unqualified providers.

Should I choose an independent agent or a captive agent?

Independent agents hold appointments with multiple carriers, so they can shop rates and pivot if one underwriter declines you. Captive agents represent a single company. For most Miami buyers — especially anyone with a health history, a 1099 income, or a Medicare decision to make — an independent agent delivers more optionality for the same $0 client cost.

Does it cost anything to work with a life and health insurance agent?

No. For life insurance, ACA Marketplace plans, and Medicare Advantage or Medigap, the carrier pays the agent's commission out of the premium. You pay the same rate whether you enroll directly or through an agent. Fee-only planners charge hourly or flat fees, but that's a different service model typically used for estate and business planning.

How much does life insurance cost in Miami for a healthy adult?

As a 2025 reference point: a healthy 30-year-old can get $250,000 of 20-year term coverage for roughly $18–$24 per month, and a healthy 45-year-old can get $500,000 of 20-year term for about $58–$75 per month. Final expense coverage for ages 45–85 generally runs $32–$95 monthly. Actual pricing depends on health, build, nicotine use, and driving record.

Can I get life insurance without a medical exam?

Yes. Several options exist — accelerated underwriting programs that use prescription and database checks instead of bloodwork, simplified issue policies with a short health questionnaire, and guaranteed issue policies for ages 50–85 with no health questions at all. Guaranteed issue carries the highest cost per $1,000 of coverage, so it should be a fallback, not a first stop.

What should I ask an agent before I sign an application?

Ask four questions: (1) Which carriers can you submit this to, and how many? (2) How are you compensated on each option? (3) What happens if my application is declined or rated? (4) Who handles the claim for my beneficiaries, and what's your response-time commitment? Any agent who hesitates on those is worth a second look.

Do I need a local agent, or can I work with one remotely?

Life insurance is written statewide in Florida, so remote appointments work fine — many agencies serve clients over video. Health and Medicare enrollment, however, benefits from local familiarity with which plans' networks actually include your doctors and hospitals in Miami-Dade. Confirm network inclusion in writing before you enroll.

What are the biggest red flags when choosing an agent?

Pressure to commit during the first appointment, refusal to put recommendations in writing, inability to name their carriers, claiming a policy is "guaranteed approval" when it isn't, and steering you toward a single expensive product before understanding your budget. Also watch for agents who won't discuss compensation — transparent agents volunteer it.
