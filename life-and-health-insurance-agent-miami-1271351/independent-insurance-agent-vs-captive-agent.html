The Short Answer, Up Front

When you search for a comparison of an independent insurance agent vs captive agent, you are usually asking one practical question: who will get me the best coverage at the best price, without wasting my time? A captive agent works for one insurance company and can only sell that company's products. An independent agent contracts with many carriers and can quote several companies in a single appointment.

For most people in Miami buying life insurance, ACA Marketplace health plans, or Medicare Advantage and Medigap coverage, the independent model produces better outcomes — more quotes, more underwriting flexibility, and a single point of contact for claims. The exception is when a specific captive carrier offers a unique benefit you genuinely want, such as a proprietary group discount through an employer or a bundled policy you already hold.

Below, we break down the pros and cons of each, show how pricing and commissions actually work, and explain exactly how to evaluate an agent in Miami before you sign anything.

Deep Dive: How Each Model Actually Works

1. What an Independent Agent Does

An independent agent holds appointments with multiple insurance carriers. Biscayne Bay Life & Health Insurance Group, for example, is appointed with Florida Blue, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna, Cigna, Ambetter, Molina, Oscar Health and Simply Healthcare on the health and Medicare side, plus Mutual of Omaha, Foresters Financial, Americo, John Hancock, Transamerica, North American, Nationwide and Gerber Life on the life side.

That breadth matters in three specific situations:

  • Health conditions. A client with controlled diabetes may be declined by one life carrier and approved at standard rates by another. An independent agent knows which carriers publish favorable underwriting guides for diabetes, sleep apnea, or a history of anxiety medication.
  • Medicare drug formularies. Medicare Part D and Medicare Advantage plans cover different prescription lists. An independent agent compares your actual medication list across every plan available in your Miami ZIP code.
  • ACA subsidies. Marketplace premium tax credits vary by plan metal tier and network. Comparing Bronze, Silver, and Gold side by side often changes the out-of-pocket math for a self-employed worker.

2. What a Captive Agent Does

A captive agent is an employee or exclusive contractor of a single insurer. They are typically well trained on that company's products and can sometimes access internal underwriting contacts or a proprietary discount. The limitation is structural: if the carrier's rate is not competitive, or if the applicant is declined, the agent has nothing else to offer.

Captive agents are a reasonable fit if you already hold a policy with that company, want a single well-known brand, or are enrolling through an employer group plan where the carrier has been pre-selected.

3. Pricing: What You Actually Pay

This is the part most comparison articles get wrong. In both models, the consumer pays the same carrier-set premium. Commissions are built into the carrier's rate filing and paid by the insurer — not added on top by the agent. That is why a free consultation with an independent agent in Miami does not cost more than calling a captive carrier's 1-800 number.

Representative life insurance rates quoted by our agency:

  • Healthy 30-year-old, $250,000 / 20-year term: roughly $18–$24 per month
  • Healthy 45-year-old, $500,000 / 20-year term: roughly $58–$75 per month
  • Final expense / burial coverage (ages 45–85): roughly $32–$95 per month for $5,000–$25,000 of coverage
  • Medicare Supplement Plan G in Miami-Dade: typically $115–$210 per month depending on age
  • Many Medicare Advantage plans: $0 monthly premium

The difference between an independent and captive agent is not the price of the policy — it is how many prices you get to see before you choose.

4. Service After the Sale

Claims advocacy is where independent agents often pull ahead. When a beneficiary needs to file a life insurance claim, an independent agency can call the carrier, track the paperwork, and follow up on the family's behalf. The same applies to denied Medicare claims or a surprise ACA billing issue. Captive agents can do this too, but only for their own company's policies.

One practical warning: independent agents who sell a large volume of Medicare Advantage plans sometimes disappear after Annual Enrollment. Ask directly how many clients the agency serves and whether annual policy reviews are included. Our agency publishes a 24-hour response guarantee and a free annual review for every client, including policies purchased elsewhere.

The Verdict: Which Should You Choose?

Choose an independent agent if you want to compare real numbers before committing, you have any health history that could trigger a decline or rate increase, you are self-employed or a 1099 contractor without employer benefits, you are turning 65 and need Medicare Advantage and Medigap compared side by side, or you want one person to call for life, health, and Medicare questions.

Choose a captive agent if you already own a policy with that specific carrier and want it serviced in-house, you are enrolling through an employer group plan that has pre-selected the carrier, or a proprietary benefit (like a loyalty dividend or bundled discount) is genuinely worth giving up comparison shopping.

In practice, the vast majority of Miami households we meet are better served by the independent model — not because independent agents are better people, but because the structure lets us say "this carrier will not approve you, but this one will" instead of "sorry, we can't help."

Frequently Asked Questions

Does using an independent agent cost more than going direct?

No. Premiums are filed with the state and are identical whether you buy through an independent agent, a captive agent, or directly from the carrier. The carrier pays the agent's commission out of the same premium. Your only "cost" is the appointment time.

Can an independent agent really get me a lower life insurance rate?

Yes, often 20–40% lower for the same coverage amount, because underwriting guidelines differ by carrier. A healthy 45-year-old in Miami shopping only one company might be quoted $95/month for $500,000 of 20-year term; comparing eight carriers typically surfaces $58–$75/month for the same benefit.

Is a captive agent more trustworthy because of the big brand name?

Brand recognition is not the same as suitability. Large carriers have strong reputations but also stricter underwriting in some categories. Trust should be evaluated on licensing, reviews, transparency about commissions, and willingness to show you multiple quotes — not on logo size.

What happens to my policy if my independent agent closes or retires?

Your policy is a contract with the insurance carrier, not with the agent. Coverage, premiums, and cash value remain intact. The carrier will reassign your servicing or you can name a new agent of record at any time with a simple form.

Can an independent agent help with Medicare Advantage and Medigap in Miami?

Yes — and this is where independence matters most. Plan formularies, networks, and copays change every year. An independent, AHIP-certified agent can compare every Medicare Advantage and Medicare Supplement option in your Miami-Dade ZIP code using your actual prescription list, at no cost to you.

Do independent agents charge a consultation fee?

Legitimate independent agents do not charge for consultations or quotes on standard life, health, and Medicare products. If someone asks for an upfront fee before showing you a quote, walk away.

I was declined by a captive carrier. Is there still hope?

Frequently, yes. Guaranteed issue life insurance (ages 50–85, no health questions) and simplified issue final expense products exist for applicants with diabetes, heart history, or prior declines. An independent agent can identify which carrier will accept your specific situation.

Does it help to work with an agent who speaks Spanish or Haitian Creole?

Absolutely. Insurance documents are dense, and misunderstandings about exclusions, waiting periods, or subsidy eligibility are costly. Our team provides bilingual support in English, Spanish, and Haitian Creole at both our Little Havana and Kendall offices.

Conclusion: Compare Before You Commit

The independent insurance agent vs captive agent decision ultimately comes down to optionality. A captive agent gives you one answer. An independent agent gives you a set of answers and the expertise to interpret them. For life insurance, ACA Marketplace plans, and Medicare coverage in Miami, that difference regularly translates into lower premiums, faster approvals, and far less frustration.
