What to Look For: The 7 Credentials That Separate a Real Professional From a Lead Seller

Before you read a single review, run the credentials. Florida makes this unusually easy: every licensed agent's record is public. Here is the checklist our own clients use on us — and we encourage you to actually use it.

1. License Type and Status (Florida DFS)

Visit the Florida Department of Financial Services license lookup and search the agent's name. You are looking for a 2-15 license (Life, Health & Annuity). A 2-40 license covers health only — perfectly fine for a Medicare specialist, but not someone who should be selling you a whole life policy. Check two fields: status (must read Active) and disciplinary history (must be empty or fully explained). An agent who has been licensed for eleven months will not show you the same results as one licensed for fourteen years.

2. Carrier Appointments and Independence

This is the single most important question in the entire process. Ask: "How many carriers are you appointed with, and can I see the list?" A captive agent works for one brand; an independent agent typically holds 8–15 appointments. More carriers means more underwriting options — which matters enormously if you have controlled diabetes, sleep apnea, a past cancer diagnosis, or a high BMI, because carriers price those conditions very differently.

3. Local Presence and Tenure

An agent who has renewed a physical Miami address for years has something an online call center does not: neighbors, referrals, and reputation risk. If they disappear, people who know them will notice. Ask for the office address and check it on Google Maps Street View.

4. Review Quality (Not Just Quantity)

Forty reviews that all say "great service, very professional" are worth less than eight reviews that describe a specific claim, a specific denial, or a specific enrollment problem that was solved. Specificity is the hardest thing to fake.

5. Certifications Specific to the Product

Medicare is not sold the way life insurance is sold. If an agent is advising you on Medicare Advantage or a Medigap Plan G, they should be AHIP certified annually and CMS-compliant in their marketing. Ask when their certification was last renewed. For life insurance, ask whether they have completed advanced underwriting training or hold a professional membership such as NABIP.

6. Written Service Promises

Vague promises are worthless. Look for documented commitments: a stated response-time guarantee, a free annual policy review, and a claims-advocacy commitment where the agency handles the carrier calls on behalf of a surviving spouse. These are the promises that matter when you are not in a position to shop around.

7. Transparency About Compensation

An agent who cannot explain how they get paid is either new or evasive. Commissions are paid by the carrier out of premium, they are not added on top, and a good agent will tell you the approximate first-year and renewal commission structure if you ask. Independent agents typically earn 50–115% of first-year premium on term life and a smaller renewal — which is exactly why long-term agents want you to keep the policy, not just buy it.

Pricing Breakdown: What You Actually Pay an Agent in Miami

Here is the fact that surprises almost every first-time buyer: a properly structured agent relationship costs you nothing out of pocket. In Florida, life and health insurance commissions are built into the carrier's premium and paid directly to the agent by the insurance company. Two clients buying the identical policy from the same carrier pay the identical premium whether they buy direct or through an agent. You are not paying a markup.

Tier 1 — Free consultation and quote. Every reputable independent agency in Miami offers this at no cost and with no obligation. If an agency wants a fee before showing you rates, walk away.

Tier 2 — Commission-paid placement. You pay the same premium either way. The difference between a good and bad agent is not price — it is carrier selection, underwriting class placement, and whether they properly classify you as Preferred Plus versus Standard, which can swing a term premium by 30–60%.

Tier 3 — Ongoing service. Free annual policy reviews, beneficiary updates, and claims advocacy should be standard and unbilled. Some fee-only advisors charge $150–$500 for an insurance review; that model exists mainly for high-net-worth estate planning and is rarely worth it for a $250,000 term policy.

Expert Tips: Six Mistakes That Cost Miami Buyers Thousands

  1. Buying a Medicare Advantage plan because the premium is $0. A zero-premium plan with a $6,700 in-network maximum and a narrow specialist network can cost far more than a $145 Medigap Plan G if you see multiple doctors. Always compare total annual exposure, not the monthly premium.
  2. Skipping the medical exam when you can qualify for one. Fully underwritten policies can be 40–60% cheaper per $1,000 than simplified issue. Clients who choose no-exam coverage out of convenience frequently overpay for years.
  3. Naming a minor child as a direct beneficiary. In Florida, this triggers a court-supervised guardianship of the proceeds. Use a revocable living trust or a custodian under the Florida Uniform Transfers to Minors Act instead.
  4. Lapsing a term policy during a health change. Once you are diagnosed, replacing coverage gets expensive or impossible. Never cancel the old policy until the new one is issued and the first premium has cleared.
  5. Assuming your employer's group life is enough. Group coverage is typically 1–2x salary, ends when the job ends, and is not portable. For a household with a mortgage and childcare costs, that is usually a fraction of the real need.
  6. Not reviewing your ACA subsidy eligibility every year. Income changes for gig workers and 1099 contractors frequently unlock larger premium tax credits. A ten-minute annual review can move a household from $380/month to under $50/month.

The 10-minute agent interview script: 1) What is your Florida license number? 2) How many carriers are you appointed with, and may I see the list? 3) How do you get paid on this policy? 4) Who services my policy if you leave the agency? 5) What happens if my claim is denied? 6) How do you classify Preferred vs. Standard underwriting? 7) Do you charge any fee at any point? 8) What is your average response time? 9) Can I see a redacted sample illustration? 10) Will you do an annual review at no charge? Any hesitation on questions 1, 3, or 5 is your answer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I verify a life insurance agent's license in Florida?

Go to the Florida Department of Financial Services website and use the "Verify a Licensee" tool. Search by the agent's full name or license number. You are looking for an Active 2-15 (Life, Health & Annuity) license with no disciplinary actions listed. The search takes under 60 seconds and returns the license's original issue date, which tells you how long the person has actually been licensed.

Do I pay more if I buy through an agent instead of directly from the carrier?

No. Life and health insurance premiums are filed with the state and are identical whether you buy direct or through a licensed agent. The carrier pays the agent's commission out of the premium it already charges. What you gain from an agent is comparative shopping across carriers, correct underwriting classification, and help at claim time — at no extra cost.

How many five-star reviews does an agent need to be trustworthy?

Volume matters less than pattern. Look for 40 or more reviews spread across at least three years, with one or two critical reviews that received a professional public response. A long history with no negatives at all is more suspicious than a 4.7 rating with a well-handled complaint. Check Google, the Better Business Bureau, and any state complaint index where available.

What should a 20-year term life policy cost a healthy 45-year-old in Miami?

For $500,000 of coverage on a healthy 45-year-old, expect roughly $58 to $75 per month in 2025, depending on tobacco use, build, family history, and the carrier's current underwriting class. A well-connected independent agent will submit to multiple carriers and let the offers compete rather than placing you with the first one that responds.

Can I get coverage if I have diabetes, sleep apnea, or a past cancer diagnosis?

Often yes, and this is precisely where the number of carrier appointments your agent holds becomes critical. Some carriers rate controlled Type 2 diabetes at Standard, others decline it entirely. Sleep apnea with a compliant CPAP history is frequently approved at Preferred with the right carrier. Many cancers are insurable after a 5–10 year remission period depending on type and stage.

What is a free policy review, and will an agent try to replace my existing coverage?

A policy review is an analysis of coverage you already own — including policies purchased elsewhere — checking death benefit adequacy, beneficiary designations, cash value performance, and premium competitiveness. A reputable agency will tell you to keep a policy when replacing it would be a mistake, which is common with older whole life contracts that have already accumulated cash value.

Do I need to be in Miami for the appointment?

For life insurance, no — virtual appointments are available statewide across Florida. For health insurance and Medicare enrollment, in-person or video appointments are the standard, which is why local offices in Little Havana and Kendall matter. Evening video appointments are available Monday through Thursday until 8:00 PM during Medicare Annual Enrollment (October 15 – December 7) and ACA Open Enrollment (November 1 – January 15).

What happens to my policy if my agent leaves the agency?

Your policy is a contract with the insurance carrier, not the agent. It remains fully in force if the agent leaves, retires, or passes away. What changes is service quality. This is why the best agencies assign a servicing agent of record and document that in writing — ask for it before you sign.
